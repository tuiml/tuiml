Summary

Across the full suite, TuiML delivers the same predictive quality as scikit-learn. Under 10-fold cross-validation the two are statistically indistinguishable on nearly every algorithm and dataset, and where they do differ meaningfully TuiML comes out ahead, most visibly on neural networks, where scikit-learn's default MLP fails to converge on several regression datasets while TuiML trains reliably. Weka's remaining wins come from it running different algorithm variants under the same name, such as pruned decision trees and target-normalizing SVR, rather than from better implementations of the same algorithm, and on like-for-like comparisons it trails the other two. TuiML pairs this accuracy with competitive training speed and the fastest inference on most algorithms, and it was the only framework to complete every experiment in the suite without a single failure or timeout.

Methodology

Protocol

  • Datasets: 51 tabular datasets from TabArena v0.1 (38 classification, 13 regression)
  • Frameworks: TuiML, scikit-learn, Weka (python-weka-wrapper3)
  • Algorithms: only those available in all three libraries
  • Validation: stratified 10-fold cross-validation (shuffled, fixed seed), folds identical across frameworks; scores are mean ± std over the 10 folds
  • Preprocessing: shared impute + scale + one-hot; Naive Bayes uses ordinal-encoded / quantile-binned features with each library's native categorical NB
  • Hyperparameters: library defaults (Weka runs its native variants)
  • Metrics: Accuracy / R², training time, peak memory

Environment

  • TuiML: v0.1.6
  • scikit-learn: v1.8.0
  • Weka: python-weka-wrapper3 0.3.2 (Java 21)
  • Each experiment: isolated process, single-threaded
